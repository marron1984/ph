The Situation

What happened in Kumamoto

At 4:27 p.m. on July 28, 2026 (JST), a magnitude 7.1 earthquake struck the Kumamoto region of Kyushu, southwestern Japan, at a depth of about 16 km (preliminary figures). Uki City and Hikawa Town registered seismic intensity 7 — the highest level on Japan's scale — the first such observation since the 2024 Noto Peninsula earthquake.

Tsunami advisories were issued for the Ariake and Yatsushiro Seas. Buildings collapsed and fires broke out across the prefecture; a shopping mall partially collapsed after an explosion, and a paper mill in Yatsushiro reported multiple casualties. Water outages and rail suspensions continue, and the Japan Meteorological Agency warns of aftershocks up to intensity 7 for about a week.

  • 3 dead, 5 in cardiac arrest, 31 injured, at least 7 missing (July 29, Kumamoto Pref.)
  • Seismic intensity 7 in Uki & Hikawa; 6+ in Kumamoto City and 4 more towns
  • 4,744 people in 466 shelters across 36 municipalities (July 28)
  • Collapses, explosion and fires at commercial and industrial sites

Sources: Japan Meteorological Agency, Kumamoto Prefecture, NHK (as of July 29, 2026). Rescue operations are ongoing and figures may change.

Who We Are

About the International Peace-Loving People Foundation

The International Peace-Loving People Foundation (IPLPF), known in Japan as 一般財団法人 国際ピース・ラビング・ピープル財団 (Kokusai P-LP Zaidan), was established in November 2020 to contribute to world peace and human development and welfare. We work to grow the community of peace-loving people in Japan and worldwide, and to support the United Nations from the private sector.

This relief fund is part of our core mission of promoting humanitarian aid — building a society where no one is left behind. Donations are used for emergency relief and recovery support for survivors of the 2026 Kumamoto Earthquake, according to needs on the ground.
